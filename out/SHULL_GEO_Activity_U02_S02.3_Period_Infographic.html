THE JOB

Your group picks one geologic period and makes an infographic about it. An infographic tells its story with pictures, a map, and short labels, not paragraphs. The menu on page 2 lists every period you can choose from so you can compare before you decide. Page 3 shows an example setup. The infographic has to be in color.

WHAT GOES ON THE INFOGRAPHIC

  1. 1Title. The name of your period, large enough to read from across the room. Add when it started and ended, in millions of years ago (mya).
  2. 2Timeline bar. A strip showing where your period sits in its era, with your period highlighted.
  3. 3Key Facts zone. Climate: warm or cold, wet or dry, ice or no ice. Continents: a colored map of where the continents were, each one labeled.
  4. 4Organisms. At least 3 important living things that evolved or first appeared in your period (the first dinosaurs, the first flowering plants). Each gets a picture, its name, and a one-line caption on why it matters.
  5. 5Short text only. Labels and one-line captions. No paragraphs.
  6. 6Color. The whole infographic is in color, not pencil or pen alone.

PALEOZOIC ERA · 539 TO 252 MYA
Cambrian
539 to 485 mya
 Warm. Shallow seas flooded the edges of the continents. The Cambrian explosion: first animals with hard shells and eyes. Trilobites, Anomalocaris. All life is in the ocean. Gondwana, a big continent mostly in the south. Smaller continents scattered near the equator.
Ordovician
485 to 444 mya
ENDS: MASS EXTINCTION
 Warm with very high sea level, then an ice age near the end. First land plants (small, mossy). Big coral reefs. Fish with no jaws. Ohio's state fossil, the trilobite Isotelus, is from this period. Gondwana over the South Pole. Ohio sat under a warm shallow sea.
Silurian
444 to 419 mya
 Warm. Ice melted and seas rose again. First plants with stems that carry water (Cooksonia). First fish with jaws. Sea scorpions. Two northern continents, Laurentia and Baltica, collide and close an ocean between them.
Devonian
419 to 359 mya
ENDS: MASS EXTINCTION
 Mostly warm. Cooling toward the end. The Age of Fishes: armored Dunkleosteus, early sharks. First forests. First four-legged animals; Tiktaalik, a fish with leg-like fins. Dunkleosteus fossils come from the Cleveland Shale. North America and Europe joined into one northern continent. Gondwana still in the south.
Carboniferous
359 to 299 mya
 Warm and wet with huge swamps. Ice sheets in the far south late in the period. Swamp forests that became coal. First reptiles. Dragonfly-like insects with wings about 2 ft across. Eastern Ohio's coal formed in these swamps. Gondwana and the northern continent collide. Pangaea starts to form.
Permian
299 to 252 mya
ENDS: MASS EXTINCTION
 Hot and dry. Deserts in the middle of the land. Dimetrodon and other mammal relatives. Conifer trees. Beetles. Ends with the worst mass extinction ever: about 90% of ocean species gone. One supercontinent: Pangaea.
MESOZOIC ERA · 252 TO 66 MYA
Triassic
252 to 201 mya
ENDS: MASS EXTINCTION
 Hot, mostly dry. No ice at the poles. First dinosaurs. First mammals (small). First pterosaurs. Early turtles. Ichthyosaurs in the sea. Pangaea, still in one piece. It begins to crack apart near the end.
Jurassic
201 to 145 mya
 Warm and humid. No ice caps. Giant long-necked dinosaurs. Stegosaurus. First birds (Archaeopteryx). Plesiosaurs in the sea. Pangaea splits into a northern and a southern continent. The Atlantic Ocean starts to open.
Cretaceous
145 to 66 mya
ENDS: MASS EXTINCTION
 Very warm. Highest sea level of the whole era; a shallow sea cut North America in two. First flowering plants. Tyrannosaurus rex and Triceratops. An asteroid hit about 66 mya and ended the non-bird dinosaurs. Continents keep splitting. South America and Africa separate. India drifts north.
CENOZOIC ERA · 66 MYA TO TODAY
Paleogene
66 to 23 mya
 Hot early on, then cooling. Ice starts to build on Antarctica. Mammals take over the empty spaces left by the dinosaurs. First whales, first horses, early primates. Looks closer to today. India crashes into Asia and the Himalaya begin to rise.
Neogene
23 to 2.6 mya
 Cooling and drying. Large Antarctic ice sheets. Grasslands spread, and so do grazing animals. Mastodons. Early human ancestors (Australopithecus). Almost the modern map. North and South America join at Panama about 3 mya.
Quaternary
2.6 mya to today
 Repeating ice ages. Sea level dropped each time ice grew. Ice sheets covered much of Ohio. Woolly mammoths, saber-toothed cats, and the first Homo sapiens (about 300,000 years ago). Many large mammals died out about 11,000 years ago. Same positions as today.

Climate
3 points
 3. Correct. Says warm or cold, wet or dry, and gives one specific detail such as ice, deserts, swamps, or sea level. 1 to 2. One word like "hot" with no detail, or partly incorrect. 0. Missing or wrong.
Continents map
3 points
 3. Map shows where the continents were in this period. Each continent is labeled. 1 to 2. Map is there but unlabeled, or only partly matches the period. 0. No map, or just today's map.
Organisms and pictures
5 points
 5. At least 3 organisms that first appeared in this period. Each has a labeled picture and a one-line caption on why it matters. 2 to 4. Fewer than 3, or one is from a different period, or pictures or captions are missing. 0 to 1. One organism or none.
Clear and visual
3 points
 3. Tells the story with pictures and short labels. No paragraphs. Zones are easy to find and readable from a few feet away. 1 to 2. Too much writing, or crowded and hard to follow. 0. Mostly paragraphs.
Color
3 points
 3. The whole infographic is colored, and the color helps show what goes together. 1 to 2. Color in some parts only. 0. Mostly black and white.
