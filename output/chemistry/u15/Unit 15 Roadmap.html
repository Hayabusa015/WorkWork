Nuclear Chemistry

Big Idea

Chemical reactions rearrange electrons. Nuclear reactions change the nucleus itself and can turn one element into another. Students explain why some nuclei are unstable, write nuclear equations, use half-life to track decay, and weigh the benefits and risks of nuclear technology.

SectionI Can… (Learning Targets)
15.1
Nuclear Stability & Radioactivity
Why some nuclei break down
  • I can explain how the ratio of neutrons to protons affects nuclear stability.
  • I can describe radioactivity as an unstable nucleus breaking down.
15.2
Types of Radioactive Decay
Alpha, beta & gamma
  • I can compare alpha, beta, and gamma by charge, mass, and penetration.
  • I can identify what material stops each type of radiation.
15.3
Nuclear Equations
Conserving mass number & charge
  • I can balance a nuclear equation by conserving mass number and charge.
  • I can identify the missing particle in a nuclear equation.
15.4
Half-Life
Fraction remaining over time
  • I can calculate how much of a sample is left after a number of half-lives.
  • I can read a decay graph to find a half-life.
15.5
Fission & Fusion
Splitting and joining nuclei
  • I can compare fission and fusion and explain where the energy comes from.
  • I can describe how a chain reaction is controlled in a nuclear power plant.
15.6
Applications, Risks & Benefits
Medicine, energy, dating & safety
  • I can describe uses of radioisotopes in medicine, energy, and dating.
  • I can weigh the benefits and risks of a nuclear technology using evidence.
