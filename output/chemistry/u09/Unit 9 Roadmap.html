Thermochemistry

· High School
5 sections · Grades 10–12
Big Idea

Every chemical and physical change moves energy. Students track heat flowing into and out of a system, tell endothermic from exothermic changes, and measure heat with q = mcΔT.

Builds On
  • U7 · S7.4 Mole Conversions
  • U8 · S8.2 Balancing Equations
  • Reference packet: specific heats, equations
SectionI Can… (Learning Targets)
9.1
Energy & Heat
Temperature, heat & specific heat
  • I can tell the difference between temperature and heat.
  • I can explain what specific heat means and why water warms up slowly.
  • I can calculate heat gained or lost with q = mcΔT.
9.2
Endothermic & Exothermic Processes
System, surroundings & heat flow
  • I can identify the system and the surroundings in a process.
  • I can classify a change as endothermic or exothermic from temperature data.
  • I can read an energy diagram to tell whether a reaction absorbs or releases energy.
9.3
Enthalpy
ΔH & thermochemical equations
  • I can explain what the sign of ΔH tells about a reaction.
  • I can write a thermochemical equation that includes ΔH.
  • I can use a thermochemical equation to find the heat for a given amount of reactant.
9.4
Calorimetry
Measuring heat in the lab
  • I can explain how a calorimeter measures heat from the temperature change of water.
  • I can calculate the heat released or absorbed from calorimetry data.
  • I can use heat lost = heat gained to find the specific heat of a metal.
9.5
Spontaneity EXT
Enthalpy, entropy & free energy
  • I can describe entropy as the spreading out of energy and matter.
  • I can predict whether entropy increases or decreases in a change.
  • I can use the signs of ΔH and ΔS to decide whether a reaction is spontaneous.
