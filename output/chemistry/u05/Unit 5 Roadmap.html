Molecular Structure & Intermolecular Forces

· High School
6 sections · Grades 10–12
Big Idea

A molecule's shape decides how it behaves. Students draw Lewis structures, use them to predict a molecule's 3-D shape and polarity, and explain how the forces between molecules set properties like boiling point.

Builds On
  • U4 · S4.3 Covalent Bonding
  • U4 · S4.6 Bond Polarity
  • Reference packet: VSEPR geometry, electronegativity table
SectionI Can… (Learning Targets)
5.1
Lewis Electron Structures
Count, connect, fill, check
  • I can draw Lewis structures, including molecules with double and triple bonds.
  • I can check a Lewis structure by counting total valence electrons and octets.
5.2
VSEPR Theory
Electron groups push apart
  • I can explain that electron groups around a central atom spread as far apart as possible.
  • I can count bonding groups and lone pairs on a central atom and write its AXE notation.
5.3
Molecular Geometry
Shapes and bond angles
  • I can predict a molecule's shape and bond angle from its Lewis structure.
  • I can explain why lone pairs squeeze bond angles, as in water and ammonia.
5.4
Molecular Polarity
Bond polarity plus shape
  • I can decide whether a molecule is polar by checking its bonds and its shape.
  • I can explain why CO2 is nonpolar even though its bonds are polar.
5.5
Intermolecular Forces
London, dipole–dipole & hydrogen bonding
  • I can identify the intermolecular forces a substance has and explain where each comes from.
  • I can tell a bond inside a molecule from a force between molecules.
5.6
Structure & Physical Properties
Why boiling points differ
  • I can predict which of two substances has the higher boiling point from its intermolecular forces.
  • I can use boiling-point data to compare the strength of intermolecular forces.
