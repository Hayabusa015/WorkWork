Electrons & Atomic Theory

· High School
5 sections · Grades 10–12
Big Idea

An atom's electrons decide how it behaves. Students move from Bohr's orbits to energy levels, sublevels and orbitals, write where every electron goes, and connect electrons dropping between levels to the light atoms give off.

SectionI Can… (Learning Targets)
2.1
The Bohr Model
Electrons in fixed energy levels
  • I can describe the Bohr model and what it added to Rutherford's model.
  • I can draw Bohr diagrams for the first 20 elements.
  • I can explain why the Bohr model works for hydrogen but not for larger atoms.
2.2
Energy Levels, Sublevels & Orbitals
Where electrons can be found
  • I can describe the difference between an energy level, a sublevel, and an orbital.
  • I can state how many orbitals the s, p, d, and f sublevels have and how many electrons each holds.
  • I can find how many electrons an energy level can hold.
2.3
Electron Configurations
Aufbau, Pauli & Hund
  • I can write longhand and noble-gas electron configurations.
  • I can draw orbital diagrams using the Aufbau principle, the Pauli exclusion principle, and Hund's rule.
  • I can use the s, p, d, and f blocks of the periodic table to find the filling order.
2.4
Electron Stability & Valence Electrons
Valence electrons, the octet & ions
  • I can count valence electrons from a configuration or from the periodic table.
  • I can explain why the noble gases are stable and unreactive.
  • I can predict the ion a main-group atom forms to reach a stable octet.
2.5
Atomic & Electron Spectra
Light, energy & line spectra
  • I can explain how electrons moving between energy levels absorb or give off light.
  • I can rank colors of light by wavelength, frequency, and energy.
  • I can identify an element by matching its line spectrum to known spectra.
