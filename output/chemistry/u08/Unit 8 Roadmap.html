Chemical Reactions

· High School
4 sections · Grades 10–12
Big Idea

A chemical equation is a sentence about a reaction: what goes in, what comes out, and in what ratio. Students write and balance equations so mass is conserved, sort reactions into five types, and use those types to predict products.

SectionI Can… (Learning Targets)
8.1
Reading & Writing Chemical Equations
Symbols, states & evidence of reaction
  • I can translate a word equation into a chemical equation and back.
  • I can use the state symbols (s), (l), (g), and (aq).
  • I can list evidence of a reaction: gas, color change, precipitate, or temperature change.
8.2
Balancing Equations
Conservation of mass
  • I can balance an equation with coefficients, never by changing subscripts.
  • I can explain how a balanced equation shows conservation of mass.
  • I can check a balanced equation by counting atoms of each element on both sides.
8.3
Reaction Types
Five common reaction types
  • I can classify a reaction as synthesis, decomposition, single replacement, double replacement, or combustion.
  • I can recognize each type from the pattern of its reactants and products.
  • I can write a balanced equation for the complete combustion of a hydrocarbon.
8.4
Predicting Products
Activity series & solubility rules
  • I can predict the products of a reaction from its type and balance the equation.
  • I can use the activity series to decide whether a single-replacement reaction happens.
  • I can use the solubility grid to decide whether a double-replacement reaction forms a precipitate.
How Learning Is Measured
  • Daily practice that builds from basic to multi-step problems
  • Friday quizzes (versions A–D)
  • Unit test, Day 1: about 25 conceptual multiple-choice questions
  • Unit test, Day 2: free-response problems, work shown
  • Quizzes may include a short naming or formula review
