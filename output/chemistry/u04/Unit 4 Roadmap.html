Chemical Bonding

· High School
6 sections · Grades 10–12
Big Idea

Atoms bond because bonded atoms are more stable than separate ones. Students use valence electrons and electronegativity to decide whether atoms transfer, share, or pool electrons, and connect each bond type to the properties of the substance it makes.

SectionI Can… (Learning Targets)
4.1
Why Atoms Bond
Stability, energy & the octet rule
  • I can explain that atoms bond because bonded atoms have lower energy than separate ones.
  • I can use the octet rule to predict how many electrons an atom gains, loses, or shares.
4.2
Ionic Bonding
Electron transfer & ionic compounds
  • I can describe an ionic bond as the attraction between oppositely charged ions formed by electron transfer.
  • I can connect ionic bonding to high melting points, brittle crystals, and conducting when melted or dissolved.
4.3
Covalent Bonding
Shared electron pairs
  • I can describe a covalent bond as a pair of electrons shared between two nonmetal atoms.
  • I can tell single, double, and triple bonds apart by the number of shared pairs.
4.4
Metallic Bonding
A sea of electrons
  • I can describe metallic bonding as metal cations in a sea of delocalized electrons.
  • I can use that model to explain why metals conduct and can be hammered into shape.
4.5
Electronegativity & Bond Type
Using EN difference to classify bonds
  • I can calculate the electronegativity difference between two bonded atoms.
  • I can classify a bond as nonpolar covalent, polar covalent, or ionic from that difference.
4.6
Bond Polarity
Partial charges & dipole arrows
  • I can draw a dipole arrow on a polar bond and label its δ+ and δ− ends.
  • I can rank bonds from least to most polar.
