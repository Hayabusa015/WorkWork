Reaction Rates & Equilibrium

Big Idea

Reactions happen when particles collide hard enough and in the right orientation. Students explain what speeds reactions up, then study reversible reactions that reach equilibrium and predict how a system at equilibrium responds when it is disturbed.

Collision Theory
Energy & orientation
  • I can explain that particles must collide with enough energy and the right orientation to react.

Factors Affecting Reaction Rate
What speeds reactions
  • I can predict how temperature, concentration, surface area, and a catalyst change reaction rate.
  • I can explain each of those effects with collision theory.
  • I can interpret reaction-rate data from a graph or table.

13.3
Chemical Equilibrium
Dynamic balance
  • I can explain that at equilibrium the forward and reverse rates are equal and the reaction has not stopped.
  • I can explain why equal rates do not mean equal amounts of reactants and products.
  • I can recognize equilibrium on a concentration graph.
C.IM.1
13.4
Equilibrium Shifts
Stresses on a system
  • I can predict which way an equilibrium shifts when a reactant or product is added or removed.
  • I can predict the shift caused by a temperature change, using ΔH.
  • I can predict the shift from a pressure change in gases.

13.5
Le Châtelier's Principle
Equilibrium in real systems
  • I can state Le Châtelier's principle in my own words.
  • I can apply it to a real system, such as making ammonia or carrying oxygen in blood.
  • I can explain why a catalyst does not shift an equilibrium.
