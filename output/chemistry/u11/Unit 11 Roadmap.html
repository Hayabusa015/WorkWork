Gases

· High School
6 sections · Grades 10–12
Big Idea

Gas particles are far apart and always moving, and that one model explains how gases behave. Students relate pressure, volume, temperature and amount, and use the gas laws to predict what a gas does when one of them changes.

Builds On

SectionI Can… (Learning Targets)
11.1
Kinetic Molecular Theory
The particle model of a gas
  • I can state the main assumptions of kinetic molecular theory.
  • I can use the theory to explain gas pressure and how temperature affects particle speed.
11.2
Gas Pressure & Variables
Pressure units & Kelvin temperature
  • I can convert between pressure units: atm, mmHg, and kPa.
  • I can convert Celsius to Kelvin and explain why gas laws need Kelvin.
11.3
Boyle's, Charles's & Gay-Lussac's Laws
Two variables at a time
  • I can use Boyle's, Charles's, and Gay-Lussac's laws to find a changed variable.
  • I can identify each law as a direct or inverse relationship from a graph or data table.
11.4
Combined Gas Law
P, V & T change together
  • I can use the combined gas law when pressure, volume, and temperature all change.
  • I can show how each simple gas law comes from the combined law.
11.5
Avogadro's Law & Molar Volume
Moles, volume & STP
  • I can use Avogadro's law to relate gas volume to moles.
  • I can use molar volume (22.4 L at STP) to convert between liters and moles.
11.6
Ideal Gas Law
PV = nRT
  • I can solve for any variable in the ideal gas law, choosing R to match the units.
  • I can explain why real gases stray from ideal behavior at high pressure and low temperature.
