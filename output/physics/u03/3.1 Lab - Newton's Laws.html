GOALS

  • Measure the acceleration of a coasting cart on a level track and report it as a mean with an uncertainty.
  • Measure the small force it takes to tow the cart at constant velocity, and connect it to friction.
  • Graph force against acceleration and read the cart’s mass from the slope, then check it against the balance.
  • Measure both forces in an interaction at the same instant with two force sensors and compare them. Record every value as you take it, with units and a sign.

PURPOSE

You are going to test all three of Newton’s laws with numbers instead of taking them on faith. Day 1 is the first and second laws. Day 2 is the third law, with two force sensors pulling on each other.

BACKGROUND

Net force, not force. Newton’s laws are about the net force ∑F, the sum of every force on the object with signs included. A moving object does not need a force to keep moving, only to change its velocity. In every part, positive is the direction you push or pull the cart. A force or acceleration with no sign is incomplete.

NEWTON’S FIRST LAW
if ∑F = 0, then a = 0  ·  velocity stays constant, including at zero

Mass is the slope. The cart records the pull on its force sensor and its own acceleration at the same moment. On a graph of force (y) against acceleration (x), the points fall on a line whose slope is the mass.

NEWTON’S SECOND LAW · AND THE SLOPE OF A FORCE–ACCELERATION GRAPH
∑F = ma  ·  N = kg·m/s²     slope = ΔF ÷ Δa = m  ·  N ÷ (m/s²) = kg

Forces come in pairs. When A pulls on B, B pulls on A with a force of the same size in the opposite direction. The two forces act on different objects, so they never cancel. On Day 2 a plus on one sensor and a minus on the other is what “opposite” looks like in data.

NEWTON’S THIRD LAW
FA on B = −FB on A
MEAN AND UNCERTAINTY (HALF THE RANGE)
value = mean ± ( largest − smallest ) ÷ 2
PERCENT ERROR
percent error = ( | experimental − accepted | ÷ accepted ) × 100
PERCENT DIFFERENCE — WHEN THERE IS NO ACCEPTED VALUE
percent difference = ( | A − B | ÷ [ ( A + B ) ÷ 2 ] ) × 100

SAFETY

  • A cart that rolls off the end of the track falls to the floor. Put an end stop on both ends of the track before the first run, and catch the cart by hand before it reaches the stop.
  • Fingers get pinched between a bumper and the front of a force sensor. In Part C, hold each sensor by its body, never by its front face, and keep fingers out of the gap.
  • A hanging mass can drop when the string slips or the cart reaches the pulley. If you do the extension, keep hands and feet out from under the mass and stop the cart before the mass reaches the floor.
  • Pull smoothly and gently. A hard yank makes the cart outrun your hand and slam into the end stop. Nothing in this lab needs a big force.
  • Keep strings off the floor and out of walkways when you are not using them.

U3 / S3.1 · NEWTON’S LAWS LAB · PROCEDURE

MATERIALS

Per group: Go Direct Sensor Cart · Dynamics Track with an end stop on each end · device with Graphical Analysis · balance · one cart mass bar · string, about 40 cm · Day 2 only: one Go Direct force sensor with its hook and bumper · Extension only: track pulley and hanging masses

PROCEDURE

Part A — First Law: No Net Force, No Acceleration DAY 1 · ABOUT 20 MIN

  1. Set the track on the bench with an end stop on each end. Level it.
    • Set the cart at rest in the middle. If it starts to roll, shim the low end with folded paper until it stays put.
  2. Mass the cart on the balance and record it to 0.001 kg. Turn the cart on and connect it to Graphical Analysis. Display velocity, acceleration and force against time.
  3. With nothing touching the hook, zero the force sensor. Do this before every run, in every part.
  4. Push trials, three times:
    • Start data collection. Give the cart one short push and let go. Let it coast for about 2 s, then catch it before the end stop.
    • On the v–t graph, select only the stretch after your hand left the cart. Leave out the push and the catch. Apply a linear fit and record the slope as a, with its sign.
  5. Tow trials, three times:
    • Tie the string to the force sensor hook. Zero the sensor with the string hanging slack.
    • Start data collection and tow the cart by the string, level with the track, at a slow, steady pace for about 1 m.
    • Select the stretch where velocity is flat. Record the mean force and the mean acceleration for that stretch.

Part B — Second Law: Mass from a Graph DAY 1 · ABOUT 20 MIN

  1. Keep the string on the hook. Positive is the direction you pull. Zero the force sensor.
  2. Start data collection. Pull the cart along the track with a series of gentle-to-firm pulls, each a little harder than the last, for about 3 s. Keep the string level. Catch the cart before the end stop.
  3. Make a graph of force against acceleration, with acceleration on the x-axis. Apply a linear fit. Record the slope (kg) and the intercept (N).
    • If the slope comes out negative, the force and acceleration signs disagree. Check the sign convention with me before you take more data.
  4. Add one mass bar. Mass the cart and bar together on the balance and record it to 0.001 kg. Repeat steps 1–3. The slope should go up by about the mass you added.
  5. Extension, if I give you a pulley: replace your hand with a string over the pulley to a small hanging mass. The force sensor still reads the string tension and the cart still reads a, so each run adds a point to your F–a graph.

Part C — Third Law: Two Sensors, One Interaction DAY 2 · ABOUT 20 MIN

  1. Connect the cart and the separate force sensor to Graphical Analysis so they record together. With nothing touching either hook, zero both sensors.
  2. Set the sign convention. Positive is toward the person holding the separate sensor, which is the direction of the pull.
    • Hook the two sensors together with about 10 cm of string. Give one gentle test tug. The cart’s sensor should read positive (the force on the cart points toward you). Your hand sensor should read negative (the force on it points back toward the cart).
    • If both read the same sign, stop and get me. One sensor’s sign has to be reversed before you go on.
  3. Trial C1, steady pull. Start data collection and pull the cart toward you along the track by the hand sensor, speeding it up smoothly for about 1 m. Catch the cart before the end stop.
  4. Trial C2, tugs. Start data collection and give the cart four or five gentle tugs, letting the string go slack between them.
  5. For each trial, show both force readings on the same graph. Record the peak force on each sensor, with sign, and sketch the two curves in your notebook with labeled axes.
  6. Trial C3, bumper push. Swap the hook on the separate sensor for its bumper. Zero both sensors. Press the bumper against the cart’s force sensor and push the cart a short way along the track. Catch it before the end stop. Record both peaks.
  7. Go back to Trial C1. Select a stretch where the cart is speeding up steadily. Record the mean force on the cart and the mean acceleration for that stretch.
  8. Use the rest of the period for the post-lab analysis.

DATA TABLES

You built these in your pre-lab. Have them open before you start data collection. Fill them in as you take each run, not at the end of the period from memory. Graphs you sketch go right below the table they belong to.

DISPOSAL AND CLEANUP — LAST FIVE MINUTES, BOTH DAYS

  1. Untie the string, take the mass bar off, and return both to the tray. Nothing goes in the trash.
  2. Turn off the cart and the separate force sensor and return them where you got them.
  3. Leave the track flat on the bench with both end stops on. Pulley and hanging masses go back in their box.
  4. Wash all glassware with the Alconox solution in the blue spray bottle. Spray inside, scrub with a brush, and rinse thoroughly — soap left in a beaker changes the next measurement made in it.
  5. Rinse with distilled water and invert everything on the rack to dry.
  6. Wipe your bench, push in your stool, and wash your hands. Goggles come off last, once the whole room is finished — not once you are.
