FAILURE POINTS

  • Force sensor not zeroed. Zero with nothing pulling on the hook before every run. A leftover offset shifts the Part B intercept and wrecks the Part A tow force.
  • Track not level. A tilt adds a slope component of gravity that swamps friction in Part A. Students check a cart at rest, then compare a gentle push each way.
  • Sign convention. If the cart reports a pull and a forward acceleration with opposite signs, the F–a slope is negative. In Part C, two sensors hooked face to face both report a pull with the same sign, because each measures the force on its own hook along its own axis. That is expected. Reversing the separate sensor puts both in track coordinates. After that, equal size at every instant with opposite sign is the third law.
  • Tow force in the noise. About 0.03 N is close to the sensor’s noise. If a group’s tow readings scatter around zero, they use m·|a| from the coasting slope as friction (post-lab 2, 4 and 6 allow this).
  • Fit region. In Part A students fit across the push and the catch, and the slope is meaningless. Only the coasting stretch counts.
  • String at an angle. A string pulled up and away puts part of the pull vertical. Keep it level with the track.
  • Force sensor range. The ±10 N / ±50 N range switch belongs to the older Vernier Dual-Range Force Sensor. Your sensor model is not confirmed. If yours have no switch, ignore this. If they do, ±10 N suits this lab.

Alternatives and backups

Two sensor carts colliding. With 8 working carts and 5 in use, the 3 spares make one extra pair. Two carts meeting bumper to bumper on one track give the same mirror-image force graphs, and both carts record acceleration, so m1a1 ≈ m2a2 can be checked. Good as a demo or for a group that finishes early. A separate force sensor standing in for a cart. A force sensor mounted on a plain cart can stand in for one sensor cart’s force reading. If your separate sensors are the Force and Acceleration model, they also record acceleration. If not, the m·a check only works for the sensor cart.

PRE-LAB KEY

  1. Net force on a cart at a constant 0.40 m/s. ∑F = 0. Constant velocity means a = 0, and the first law says that only happens when the net force is zero. The push stopped acting the moment the hand left. No credit for “a small forward force keeps it going.”
  2. Slope of F against a. Mass. Units of slope: Nm/s² = kg·m/s²m/s² = kg.
  3. Force of cart on sensor when the sensor pulls directly on the cart’s hook with +2.0 N. −2.0 N, the same size, pointing back toward the cart. The two forces act on different objects. Only the +2.0 N acts on the cart, so the cart accelerates. Partial credit for −2.0 N with no reason.
  4. Part A, step 3. Zero it, with nothing pulling on the hook. For the tow, the string hangs slack.
  5. Region of v–t to fit. Only the coasting stretch after the hand leaves the cart. Leave out the push and the catch.
  6. Accepted value in Part B. The balance mass. With a bar on, mass the cart and bar together on the balance. Do not add a stamped value.
  7. Part C, step 2. Each sensor measures the force on its own hook along its own axis, and the two face each other, so both report a pull the same way. After one is reversed, the cart reads positive and the hand sensor negative.
  8. Safety. End stops on both ends of the track. Hold each sensor by its body, with fingers off the front face and out of the gap. Eye protection per the room’s standing rule.
  9. Data tables drawn. Full credit = 3 tables drawn in ink, with units in the row labels.

POST-LAB KEY — FROM THE SAMPLE DATA

  1. Coasting a with uncertainty. −0.096 ± 0.008 m/s². Tiny next to the 1–2 m/s² of Part B, so close to zero, but the range −0.104 to −0.088 does not include zero. Rolling friction points opposite the motion, which is the negative direction. “Human error” earns nothing.
  2. m|a| against tow force. (0.305 kg)(0.096 m/s²) = 0.029 N, against +0.03 ± 0.02 N. While coasting, friction is the only horizontal force, so f = m|a| (second law). While towing at constant velocity a = 0, so ∑F = 0 and the tow force equals friction (first law). They agree within the tow uncertainty. Full credit also for a group that says its tow reading was lost in noise and picks m|a| as the better friction, because it comes from a clean fitted slope.
  3. Percent error for each slope. 4.3% and 2.6%. Full work under Part B of the worked sample analysis, page 3. Units carried in every step.
  4. Slope rise and intercept. Up 0.463 kg against 0.497 kg added, 6.8% low. The intercept (+0.041 N, +0.046 N) is the force reading at a = 0, the force that only balances friction. It is comparable to the Part A friction of about 0.03 N, higher by 0.01–0.02 N, which is inside the tow uncertainty. The mass changed, so the slope changed.
  5. Percent difference of peaks; signs. 2.8%, 3.0%, 3.3%. The opposite signs mean opposite directions, and the mirror-image curves show the sizes are equal at every instant, not only at the peak. The forces don’t cancel because one acts on the cart and the other acts on the hand sensor. Forces cancel only when they act on the same object.
  6. m·a against ∑F = F − f. +0.482 N against +0.49 N, 1.6% apart. The force sensor reads only the pull. Friction also acts on the cart, opposite the motion, so ∑F = F − f. Either Part A friction value (+0.03 N tow, or 0.029 N from m|a|) gives +0.49 N. Common wrong answer: comparing m·a with 0.52 N directly. Partial credit if the friction reason is given.
  7. Unzeroed sensor reading +0.15 N. Every force reads 0.15 N high, so the intercept rises by 0.15 N and the slope does not change. Their mass and percent error are unaffected. Common wrong answer: “the mass comes out too big.” No credit without the slope-against-intercept reason.
  8. Track tilted downhill. Gravity now has a component along the motion. The slope becomes less negative, zero, or even positive, and it hides friction. Part A step 1: the cart-at-rest check and the gentle push each way.
  9. Connection to 1.3. Part A step 4: the linear-fit slope of v–t is the acceleration. The area under v–t is displacement, in metres.
