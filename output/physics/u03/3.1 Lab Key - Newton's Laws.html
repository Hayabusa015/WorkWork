FAILURE POINTS

  • Force sensor not zeroed. Zero with nothing on the hook before every run. A leftover offset shifts the Part B intercept and wrecks the Part A tow force, which is only a few hundredths of a newton.
  • Track not level. A tilt adds a slope component of gravity that swamps friction in Part A. Check that a cart at rest stays put before Part A starts.
  • Sign convention. If the cart software reports a pull and a forward acceleration with opposite signs, the F–a slope is negative. In Part C, two identical sensors hooked face to face read the same sign until one is reversed. Reversing one puts both in track coordinates, and then the minus sign is the “opposite.”
  • Fit region. In Part A students fit across the push and the catch, and the slope is meaningless. Only the coasting stretch counts.
  • String at an angle. A string pulled up and away puts part of the pull vertical. Keep it level with the track.
  • Force sensor range. Some Vernier force sensors have a ±10 N / ±50 N range switch. Unconfirmed for your Go Direct force sensors. Check yours. If there is one, ±10 N is the better setting here, since nothing in this lab is near 10 N.

Alternatives and backups

Two sensor carts colliding. With 8 working carts and 5 in use, the 3 spares make one extra pair. Two carts meeting bumper to bumper on one track give the same mirror-image force graphs, and both carts have accelerometers, so m1a1 ≈ m2a2 can be checked. Good as a demo or for a group that finishes early. A separate force sensor standing in for a cart. A force sensor mounted on a plain cart can stand in for one sensor cart’s force reading, but that cart has no accelerometer, so the m·a check only works for the sensor cart.

PRE-LAB KEY

  1. Net force on a cart at a constant 0.40 m/s. ∑F = 0. Constant velocity means a = 0, and the first law says that only happens when the net force is zero. The push stopped acting the moment the hand left. No credit for “a small forward force keeps it going.”
  2. Slope of F against a. Mass. Units of slope: Nm/s² = kg·m/s²m/s² = kg.
  3. Force of cart on sensor when the sensor pulls with +2.0 N. −2.0 N, the same size, pointing back toward the cart. The two forces act on different objects. Only the +2.0 N acts on the cart, so the cart accelerates. Partial credit for −2.0 N with no reason.
  4. Part A, step 3. Zero it, with nothing touching the hook. For the tow, the string hangs slack.
  5. Region of v–t to fit. Only the coasting stretch after the hand leaves the cart. Leave out the push and the catch.
  6. Accepted value in Part B. The balance mass. With a bar on, mass the cart and bar together on the balance. Do not add a stamped value.
  7. Part C, step 2. Cart sensor positive, hand sensor negative. If both show the same sign, stop and get the teacher.
  8. Safety. End stops on both ends of the track. Hold each sensor by its body, with fingers off the front face and out of the gap.
  9. Data tables drawn. Full credit = 3 tables drawn in ink, with units in the row labels.

POST-LAB KEY — FROM THE SAMPLE DATA

  1. Coasting a with uncertainty. −0.096 ± 0.008 m/s². Tiny next to the 1–2 m/s² of Part B, so close to zero, but the range −0.104 to −0.088 does not include zero. Rolling friction points opposite the motion, which is the negative direction. “Human error” earns nothing.
  2. m|a| against tow force. (0.305 kg)(0.096 m/s²) = 0.029 N, against 0.034 N. While coasting, friction is the only horizontal force, so friction = m|a| (second law). While towing at constant velocity a = 0, so ∑F = 0 and the tow force equals friction (first law). Both measure the same friction. Agreement within about 0.005 N is good for forces this small.
  3. Percent error for each slope. 4.3% and 2.6%. Full work under Part B of the worked sample analysis, page 3. Units carried in every step.
  4. Slope rise and intercept. Up 0.463 kg against 0.497 kg added, 6.8% low. The intercept (+0.041 N, +0.046 N) is the force reading at a = 0, which is the force that only balances friction. It matches the Part A tow force of about 0.03 N. The mass changed, so the slope changed.
  5. Percent difference of peaks; signs. 2.8%, 3.0%, 3.3%. The opposite signs mean opposite directions. The forces don’t cancel because one acts on the cart and the other acts on the hand sensor. Forces cancel only when they act on the same object.
  6. m·a against F − Ftow. 0.482 N against 0.486 N, 0.8% apart. The force sensor reads only the pull. Friction also acts on the cart, opposite the motion, so ∑F = pull − friction. Common wrong answer: comparing m·a with 0.52 N directly. Partial credit if the friction reason is given.
  7. Unzeroed sensor reading +0.15 N. Every force reads 0.15 N high, so the intercept rises by 0.15 N and the slope does not change. Their mass and percent error are unaffected. Common wrong answer: “the mass comes out too big.” No credit without the slope-against-intercept reason.
  8. Track tilted downhill. Gravity now has a component along the motion. The slope becomes less negative, zero, or even positive, and it hides friction. Part A step 1, the level check with the cart at rest.
  9. Connection to 1.3. Part A step 4: the linear-fit slope of v–t is the acceleration. The area under v–t is displacement, in metres.
