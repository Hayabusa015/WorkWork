### U1 · The Universe & Solar System — 6 sections
`1.1` The Big Bang · `1.2` Formation of the Universe & Early Stars · `1.3` The Scale of the
Universe · `1.4` Formation of a Solar System · `1.5` Our Star · `1.6` Birth of the Earth

### U2 · Geologic Time — 5 sections
`2.1` The Geologic Time Scale · `2.2` Eons, Eras, Epochs & Periods · `2.3` Period-by-Period Deep
Dive · `2.4` Supercontinents Through Time · `2.5` Mass Extinction Events

> **Distinctive structure.** `2.3` is roughly one geologic period per day, each covering that
> period's geology, what Earth looked like, dominant life, and notable organisms.
> **Running student project:** students add to a personal geologic timeline throughout the unit,
> section by section.

### U3 · Rocks & Minerals — 5 sections
`3.1` Minerals & Mineral ID (Mineral Lab) · `3.2` Igneous Rocks · `3.3` Sedimentary Rocks ·
`3.4` Metamorphic Rocks · `3.5` The Rock Cycle

> The only genuine hands-on lab work in the course lives here. `3.2`–`3.4` run as a daily rock-type
> lab or station, one rock type per day.

### U4 · Plate Tectonics — 5 sections
`4.1` Layers of the Earth · `4.2` Continental Drift · `4.3` Paleomagnetism · `4.4` Plate Boundaries
& Convergent Types · `4.5` Hot Spots

> `4.4` covers all three convergent subtypes — continental–continental, oceanic–continental,
> oceanic–oceanic — plus divergent and transform.

### U5 · Volcanoes — 5 sections
`5.1` Magma Types & Composition · `5.2` Volcano Types & Composition · `5.3` Volcanic Explosivity
Index (VEI) · `5.4` Types of Volcanoes · `5.5` Volcanic Hazards

> **Open question 1.** `5.2` and `5.4` read as near-duplicates on the roadmap. The legacy skill
> listed them separately too — likely `5.2` is composition-driven and `5.4` is morphology (shield,
> composite, cinder cone). **Confirm before building either.** Not treated as an error.

### U6 · Earthquakes — 5 sections
`6.1` Elastic Rebound Theory · `6.2` Fault Types · `6.3` Earthquake Properties · `6.4` Seismic
Waves · `6.5` Tsunamis

> `6.3` covers focus, epicenter, and magnitude versus intensity. `6.4` covers P, S, and surface.

### U7 · Caves — 5 sections
`7.1` Karst Topography & Cave Chemistry · `7.2` Types of Caves · `7.3` Speleothems ·
`7.4` Cave Ecosystems · `7.5` Ohio Caves & Cave Safety

> `7.5` — Ohio Caverns, Seneca Caverns, and Zane Caverns are real, visitable Ohio show caves.
> Cave safety includes white-nose syndrome and why formations are not touched.

### U8 · Glacial Geology — 6 sections
`8.1` Glacier Formation & Types · `8.2` Glacial Movement · `8.3` Erosional Glacial Features ·
`8.4` Depositional Glacial Features · `8.5` Ohio's Glacial History · `8.6` Ice Cores & Climate
Evidence

> Ohio's standard (PG.GG) emphasises **process and evidence over memorising glacier-type names**,
> and specifically calls out tracing Ohio's own glacial history with real data. Keep `8.1` brief.
> `8.5` — Wisconsinan and Illinoian ice sheets, Ohio's glacial boundary line, the Great Lakes. The
> state glacial boundary map is an excellent colouring activity.
> `8.6` sets up U9.

### U9 · Oceans & Climate — 6 sections
`9.1` Ocean Floor Features · `9.2` Ocean Water Properties · `9.3` Ocean Currents · `9.4` Tides &
Waves · `9.5` Sea-Level Change · `9.6` Earth's Climate System

> Ohio has no separate oceans/climate strand — ocean features sit inside PG.IMS.CE.5 and climate
> evidence ties to PG.GG.CE.1 via ice cores. Combining them is standards-consistent.
> `9.1` ties back to U4. **`9.6` stays data-interpretation-focused, not a policy debate.**

### U10 · Earth's Resources — 6 sections
`10.1` Renewable vs. Nonrenewable Resources · `10.2` Fossil Fuels: Formation & Extraction ·
`10.3` Ohio's Energy Resources · `10.4` Water Resources · `10.5` Soil Resources · `10.6` Air
Resources & Conservation

> **Capstone placement is deliberate.** It pulls threads from the whole year: ores and fossil fuels
> back to U3, groundwater back to U7 karst, soil back to U8 weathering and glacial deposits, and the
> conservation framing closes the loop with U9.
> `10.3` — Ohio coal, natural gas and fracking, and wind, with real local data and maps.
> `10.6` works better as a debate-style organiser or decision map than a lecture.

**Section count:** 6+5+5+5+5+5+5+6+6+6 = **54**

## Course identity — CONFIRMED

**Geology is not "Chemistry with rocks."**

It primarily serves students who need a science credit to graduate, not the advanced population in
Chemistry. **Rigor is intentionally lower.** Matt cannot lecture as heavily here — the curriculum is
built *around* the students rather than delivered at them.

- Roughly 40–50 students across periods, skewing lower-level and credit-recovery.
- 50-minute period. *(CARRIED OVER from Chemistry — PROVISIONAL for Geology.)*

### Instructional philosophy

- **Visuals and diagrams over text.** Every concept gets a strong visual anchor — diagram, labelled
  image, map, model, timeline.
- **Cut, colour, and glue projects are a first-class instructional tool here, not filler.**
  Foldables, interactive notebook pages, cut-and-sequence activities, diagram labelling and
  colouring.
- **Graphic organizers are a default content-delivery and note-taking format**, more so than in
  Chemistry — flowcharts, comparison charts, fill-in diagrams, sequencing charts, cause/effect maps.
- Lower reading level and vocabulary load than Chemistry. **Teach real vocabulary and real science —
  this is not watered down to meaninglessness** — but scaffold heavily and do not assume strong
  independent reading or math.

## Activities, not formal labs — CONFIRMED

The biggest structural difference from Chemistry.

- The only genuine hands-on labs involve **rocks and minerals** — specimen identification, mineral
  property testing, rock-type stations. Those live in U3.
- Everything else is an **activity**: simulations, diagram work, modelling, cut/colour/glue projects,
  graphic organizers. **No formal lab report cycle.**
- Matt actively seeks **online simulations** where good ones exist — plate boundary simulators,
  seismic wave and epicenter location, glacier movement models, rock cycle interactives.
- A rock/mineral lab uses the lighter template — goal, brief background, procedure and ID steps,
  data table, a few analysis questions — without Chemistry's full pre-lab, safety, and disposal
  apparatus unless a real hazard is present.

## Assessment shape — CONFIRMED

Simpler and lower-stakes than Chemistry's two-day structure.

- **Single-day tests and quizzes.** No separate computational test day.
- Heavy on **multiple choice and matching**, with labelled-diagram and fill-in-the-blank items where
  appropriate.
- **Avoid multi-step calculation-heavy free response.** This course is not computation-driven.
- **Completed graphic organizers, foldables, and cut/colour/glue projects are legitimate, valued
  demonstrations of understanding** — not "extra" engagement layered on top of a real test.

## Course-specific conventions — CONFIRMED

**Icons.** Favour geology hammer, magnifying glass, mountain and volcano, rock layers, wave, compass,
timeline and clock, map. **Reserve the atom, beaker, and molecule set for Chemistry.**

**Body text.** Err toward slightly larger body text and shorter text blocks than Chemistry, given the
reading level and visual load.

**Cut/colour/glue and foldables.** Design with clear fold lines, generous cut margins, and print-safe
non-bleeding colour regions. These have to survive scissors and glue sticks in a 50-minute period.

**Asset types.** Foldables and interactive-notebook pages · cut-and-sequence activities (geologic
periods, rock cycle steps, wave types) · diagram-labelling and colouring pages · graphic organizers
as a *primary* note-taking format · timeline project pages for the U2 running timeline · map-based
activities (Ohio glacial boundary, plate boundary world map, ocean floor, Ohio energy resources).

**Specimen photography.** Rock and mineral identification photographs are **shot, never generated**.
A generated "quartz" is not quartz, and identification is the thing being assessed. Atmosphere may
be generated.

**Tone.** Same Mr. Shull voice, simpler sentence structure, lower reading level than Chemistry's
10th–11th target. Give the real scientific term, then spend more time than Chemistry does on the
plain-language explanation. **Lean on visuals to carry meaning** — if a concept can be shown in a
diagram, don't also explain it in three sentences.
