## 9. Acid naming rules as taught

1. Decide acid context first. (aq) or water means acid naming, so HCl(g) is hydrogen chloride and HCl(aq) is hydrochloric acid. "An initial H alone does not establish acid context" (slide 3 note).
2. Does the anion contain oxygen?
   - **No, binary acid:** hydro- + nonmetal root + -ic acid. Roots given: chlor-, brom-, sulfur-.
   - **Yes, oxyacid:** -ate → -ic acid; -ite → -ous acid; no hydro-; keep per- or hypo- when present.
3. Formula: one H⁺ per unit of negative anion charge (HNO3, H2SO4, H3PO4, H2S).
4. Reverse direction: -ous → -ite, -ic → -ate.

Not covered: -ide → hydro-…-ic stated as the anion-ending rule (it is implied), and examples of per-/hypo- acids, acetic acid or carbonic acid.
