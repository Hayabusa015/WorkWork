### The "why" (slide 20 and notes), judged as a high-school explanation
- Accurate and honest. Claims: 4s and 3d are almost equal in energy; moving one 4s electron makes 3d full (Cu) or half full with parallel spins (Cr); that lowers total energy slightly. It labels itself "a simplification, not a rule: it misses other exceptions" on the student page. Notes say "Do NOT say half-full and full are always more stable", separate STUDENT and TEACHER levels, and mark the deeper story (exchange, correlation) PROVISIONAL. Good.
- Counterexamples, checked: Nb [Kr] 5s1 4d4 and W regular [Xe] 4f14 5d4 6s2 (same group as Cr and Mo, which is an exception). Web access to NIST was blocked by the proxy (403), so NIST itself was NOT reached. I checked them against the `mendeleev` Python package (installed from PyPI; its dataset is derived from NIST/CRC): Nb 4d4 5s1, W 4f14 5d4 6s2, Mo 4d5 5s1, Cr 3d5 4s1, Cu 3d10 4s1, Ag 4d10 5s1, Au 4f14 5d10 6s1. All agree with the notes. Status: VERIFIED against a secondary source, not NIST directly. Mo/Ag/Au are named as untaught.
- MINOR (slide 20 notes): the counterexamples are still labelled PROVISIONAL; they can now be relabelled verified once Matt (or a NIST fetch) confirms.
- MINOR (slide 20): "That lowers the atom's total energy slightly" is asserted as fact for a student reader. Acceptable given the framing line on the slide; no change required.
