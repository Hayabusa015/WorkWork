**Do not print "the biggest meat-eating dinosaur ever."** That claim depends on which mass estimate you trust. The safe statement is "**one of the longest** meat-eating dinosaurs known." The 2014 Bristol release says Spinosaurus was longer than the largest known T. rex by more than 9 ft (about 2.7 m). That is a length claim, not a mass claim.

| Myhrvold, Sereno et al. 2024, *PLOS ONE* | The statistical method in Fabbri 2022 is unreliable with these data. **Status: unresolved.** Everyone agrees Spinosaurus lived around water and ate fish. The open question is whether it **chased prey underwater** (Ibrahim, Fabbri) or **waded and ambushed from the shore** (Hone & Holtz, Sereno, Myhrvold).

**Student-safe wording [S]:** "Spinosaurus lived along rivers and caught fish. Scientists are still arguing about whether it swam underwater to hunt or waded in the shallows like a giant heron."

## 3. Hip types

| Term | Plain-language definition [S] | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Saurischian** — "lizard-hipped" | In most of these dinosaurs, one hip bone (the pubis) points **forward and down**, like a lizard's. The group has two branches: **theropods** (meat-eaters such as T. rex, Velociraptor and Spinosaurus) and **sauropodomorphs** (long-necked plant-eaters such as Brachiosaurus and Plateosaurus). | [Smithsonian, "Dinosaur Division is All in the Hips"](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/dinosaur-division-is-all-in-the-hips-20477310/); [Saurischia (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurischia) |
| **Ornithischian** — "bird-hipped" | The pubis points **backward**, like a bird's. All are plant-eaters, including Stegosaurus, Triceratops and Ankylosaurus. | same |
| **The twist** [S] | **Birds evolved from the *lizard-hipped* group**, from small theropods. Not from the bird-hipped group. The bird-like hip evolved twice: once in ornithischians, and again separately in the theropods that led to birds. Harry Seeley named the two groups in 1888 and never claimed ornithischians were related to birds. | same |
| Teacher caution [T] | *Velociraptor* and birds are saurischians, yet their pubis points **backward**. A student who decides hip type by looking at a Velociraptor skeleton will get the wrong answer. | Saurischia (Wikipedia) |
| **Ornithoscelida** [T] | Baron, Norman & Barrett 2017 (*Nature* 543:501–506) proposed that theropods are closer to ornithischians than to sauropodomorphs, which would mean "Saurischia" is not a natural group. Langer et al. 2017 (*Nature* 551:E1–E3) re-analysed the data and recovered the traditional split, though support either way was weak. Baron et al. replied (*Nature* 551:E4–E5). **Still debated. The traditional two-group scheme remains the standard teaching model.** | [Manchester release](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/discover/news/time-to-rewrite-the-dinosaur-textbooks-not-quite-yet/); [Bristol release](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2017/november/dinosaur-textbooks.html); [SciAm blog](https://blogs.scientificamerican.com/tetrapod-zoology/ornithoscelida-rises-a-new-family-tree-for-dinosaurs/) |

## 5. Common mistakes — teacher key

| # | Mistake | Correction | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| M-1 | "Pterosaurs (pterodactyls) are flying dinosaurs." | They are **not dinosaurs**. They are their closest relatives. | [AMNH](https://www.amnh.org/explore/news-blogs/what-is-a-pterosaur); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/why-a-pterosaur-is-not-a-dinosaur-87082921) |
| M-2 | "Plesiosaurs, ichthyosaurs and mosasaurs are swimming dinosaurs." | They are **marine reptiles, not dinosaurs**. Mosasaurs are **lizards** (squamates), related to monitor lizards or snakes — debated. | [UGA GEOL 3352E](https://www.geology.uga.edu/courses/content/geol-3352e); [Mosasaurus (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosasaurus) |
| M-3 | "Dimetrodon is a dinosaur." | It is a **Permian synapsid**, more closely related to mammals. It died out roughly 60 million years before the first dinosaurs. | [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/the-dimetrodon-in-your-family-tree-54302176/); [Harvard MCZ](https://whatsinaname.hmnh.harvard.edu/dimetrodon.md); [*Evo Edu Outreach* 2009](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12052-009-0117-4) |
| M-4 | Drawing grassy plains behind dinosaurs. | Grass was **absent or rare** for most of the Mesozoic. Grass bits do appear in Late Cretaceous dinosaur dung from India (Prasad et al. 2005), but **grasslands** only spread in the Cenozoic (Strömberg 2011). Use ferns, conifers, cycads, and flowering plants in the Cretaceous. | [Nature news 2005](https://www.nature.com/news/2005/051114/full/news051114-13.html); [Strömberg 2011, *Annu Rev EPS* 39:517–544](https://www.biology.washington.edu/pubs/evolution-grasses-and-grassland-ecosystems) |
| M-5 | T. rex fighting Stegosaurus. | They **never met**. Arithmetic, re-solved: the youngest Stegosaurus is about 150 Ma (145 at the extreme) and the oldest T. rex about 68 Ma, so they are at least 77 million years apart. T. rex to today is at most 68 million years. **T. rex lived closer in time to us than to Stegosaurus.** | [AMNH, 11 Questions About Stegosaurus](https://www.amnh.org/explore/videos/dinosaurs-and-fossils/11-questions-stegosaurus) |
| M-6 | Spinosaurus fighting T. rex (*Jurassic Park III*). | They **never met**. They were at least about 26 million years apart (93.9 − 68), on different continents (Africa and North America). | §1; [Wikipedia (T. rex range)](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/T.Rex) |
| M-7 | Movie-sized Velociraptor with scales. | Real Velociraptor was about **turkey-sized** (~1.5–2 m long, mostly tail) and **had feathers**: quill knobs on its forearm (Turner, Makovicky & Norell 2007). The movie raptors are closer to *Deinonychus* in size. | [NHM myths page](https://nhm.ac.uk/discover/debunking-dinosaur-myths-and-movie-misconceptions.html); [FAO/AGRIS record, Turner 2007](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65dedbfc6eef00c2cea15bc0) |
| M-8 | Dilophosaurus with a neck frill that spits venom. | There is **no evidence** for a frill or venom. The real animal was also bigger than the movie version, about 6–7 m. | [NHM myths page](https://nhm.ac.uk/discover/debunking-dinosaur-myths-and-movie-misconceptions.html); [UT Jackson School](https://www.jsg.utexas.edu/news/?p=15512) |
| M-9 | "Bird-hipped dinosaurs became birds." | **No.** Birds came from the lizard-hipped theropods (§3). | §3 |
| M-10 | Calling *Mussaurus* a Triassic dinosaur. | It is now dated to the **Early Jurassic, about 193 Ma**. Older books say Triassic. | [Mussaurus (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mussaurus); [Sci.News 2021](https://www.sci.news/paleontology/mussaurus-patagonicus-herds-10194.html) |

| M-12 | Dinosaurs and people lived at the same time. | The non-bird dinosaurs died out 66 Ma. *Homo sapiens* is about 300,000 years old. | ICS chart; [AMNH](https://www.amnh.org/explore/videos/dinosaurs-and-fossils/11-questions-stegosaurus) |
