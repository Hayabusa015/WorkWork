## 2. Course identity — PROVISIONAL, needs confirmation

Chemistry is Matt's primary course and highest rigor. Geology is intentionally lower rigor
and built around the students rather than delivered at them. **Physics has not been placed
on that spectrum yet.**

Before building a full unit, ask:

1. Who takes Physics here — college-bound, credit-completers, or a mix?
2. Is it algebra-based throughout, or does any trig appear?
3. Does it run parallel to Chemistry in rigor, or between Chemistry and Geology?
4. How much lab time is realistic, and what equipment is actually in the room?

One question at a time if the answer matters for the current task. Otherwise make a
PROVISIONAL call and say so in the teacher notes.

## 4. Shared standards — CARRIED OVER

These come from Chemistry and hold across the SHULL system unless Matt says otherwise.

**Practice progression.** 2–4 accessible → 3–4 standard → 1–2 difficult multi-step →
1 connection problem linking to an earlier topic.

**Notes.** Cornell format. Heavy scaffolding early in the year, tapering. The deck-to-notes
connection must be obvious. Must-write material visually marked. Concise bullets, not
paragraphs. Worked example always followed by a completed solution.

**Slides.** Visual and purposeful. Bullets, not paragraphs. Frequent diagrams, data tables,
and graphs. Section dividers show location in the unit. One must-write cue per slide.
Every worked example followed by its solution slide.

**Handouts.** Modern, printer-friendly, grayscale-safe, ~11pt body, clear hierarchy,
Name / Date / Period, unit and section identified in the header.

**Answer support.** Numeric answers in brackets for self-checking. No printed answers for
written-response, vocabulary, explanation, or graph-reading items. Calculators allowed.
Units required in every dimensional-analysis step.

**Differentiation.** Modified (M) / Challenge framework, same as the other two courses.

**Gradebook weights.** Chemistry runs Tests 55 / Quizzes 20 / Labs & Projects 15 /
Homework 7 / Binders 3. **Do not apply these to Physics without asking.** — NEEDED

## 5. Physics-specific rules — CONFIRMED as discipline standards

These are physics-content requirements, not preferences, and they apply regardless of how
the rest of the course settles.

**Units and significant figures.** Every numeric answer carries a unit. Every setup shows
units cancelling. Sig figs follow the measurement rules already taught in Chemistry — do not
re-teach them from scratch, but do enforce them.

**Vectors.** Any vector quantity gets a direction, always — magnitude alone is an incomplete
answer for displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum, and field strength. Free-body
diagrams are drawn with the object as a dot or box, one labeled arrow per force, arrows scaled
to relative magnitude, and no arrow for "motion."

**Graph interpretation is a first-class skill,** not a garnish. Students should regularly
read slope and area as physical quantities: v-t slope is acceleration, v-t area is
displacement, F-x area is work. Build at least one graph-reading item into every practice set
where kinematics, forces, or energy are in play.

**Sign conventions get stated explicitly** on every problem set that uses them. Declare the
positive direction in the directions, not implicitly through the diagram.

**Common misconceptions to write distractors against:**

- Heavier objects fall faster
- A moving object must have a net force on it
- Normal force always equals weight
- Centripetal force is a separate force acting outward
- Energy is "used up" rather than transferred
- Confusing mass with weight, speed with velocity, and heat with temperature

**Labs.** Physics labs produce a measured value with uncertainty. Require percent error
against an accepted value where one exists, and require students to name a specific source
of error — "human error" is not an answer. Follow the SHULL lab template: specific safety,
pre-lab, numbered procedure, disposal or reset, observations and data, post-lab analysis,
percent error, and a teacher preparation and key section.

## 8. Worksheets
```markdown
**No work areas on worksheets.** Physics practice sets, homework worksheets, and study
guides carry the problems and prompts only — no `.write` ruled lines and no `.math` work
boxes on the handout itself. Students do all calculation and reasoning work in their lab
notebooks, not on the page. This differs from Chemistry, where practice sets include work
boxes; flag this distinction if a request tries to reuse the Chemistry practice-set template
unmodified for Physics. Include reminders of prior knowledge and equations needed for worksheet at top of worksheet

Pre-lab and post-lab prompts on lab handouts follow the same rule already in place for
Chemistry: procedure and prompts only, no data tables or answer space — students set up
their own tables in the lab notebook as a pre-lab step.
